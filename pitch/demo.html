Margadarshak — a shift, as an airport operator would see it

Example Domestic Terminal · 4 chairs · 1h 26m elapsed

This is the output of a working dispatcher, not a mock-up. Margadarshak is a wheelchair that takes airport passengers to their gate by following a guide line laid into the terminal floor. The chair itself stays deliberately simple; everything requiring judgement — which chair, which corridor, which gate, whose battery — happens here.

The shift below was produced by running python3 -m sim.demo against an example terminal. It covers the situations that are hard to show with a model chair on a table: a passenger who scans a boarding pass and says nothing else, a gate that has not been announced yet, a gate that moves while somebody is being carried to it, a family travelling as one caravan, a call button at a gate, chairs moving towards the kerb before a rush, a corridor closed under a moving chair, and chairs returning themselves to charge.

Nothing here has carried a real passenger. The dispatcher is built and covered by 226 automated tests. The hardware is designed, drawn to 4 mm MDF and costed at ₹4,679 for two chairs — and not yet cut. It is being built for the December exhibition.
