# Chassis and body

`blueprint.pdf` is the assembly drawing: dimensioned side and front elevations of
the whole chair, with a title block and the assembly order. A3, 1:1, though every
figure that matters is written on it so printing it smaller loses nothing. That
is the sheet to work from and the one to put in front of somebody.

The other two PDFs are 1:1 cutting templates — shapes to glue onto the board and
cut round. The STLs are the same parts for a printer. Everything is generated
from `generate_chassis.py`, so no two of them can disagree.

| File | |
|---|---|
| `blueprint.pdf` | The assembly drawing — read this first |
| `mdf_template.pdf` | 1:1 cutting template, drive platform |
| `mdf_body_template.pdf` | 1:1 cutting template, wheelchair body |
| `deck.stl`, `sensor_bracket.stl`, `castor_riser_20mm.stl` | The printable alternative |

## Printable parts

Three STL files for one chair. Upload them as they are; they are already
oriented to sit on the bed and print without supports.

| File | Size | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `deck.stl` | 110 × 160 × 4 mm | The main plate. Motors zip-tied underneath, everything else bolted on top. |
| `sensor_bracket.stl` | 110 × 26 × 46 mm | Front bracket: line sensor low down, ultrasonic facing forward. |
| `castor_riser_20mm.stl` | 40 × 40 × 20 mm | Packer under the castor so the deck sits level. |

About **42 g of PLA** at 20% infill, all three together.

Everything is generated by `generate_chassis.py`. Change a number at the top of
that file and re-run it rather than editing an STL.

## Check these three before printing

The parts are built from catalogue figures, not from calipers on your actual
components. Measure these and, if they differ, change them in
`generate_chassis.py` and re-run:

| Constant | Assumed | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `WHEEL_DIA` | 65 mm | Wheel outside diameter. Sets the whole ride height. |
| `MOTOR_BODY` | 48 × 23 × 19 mm | BO gearbox body: length along its shaft, width, height. |
| `MOTOR_AXIS_FROM_BASE` | 9.5 mm | Shaft axis height above the bottom of the motor body. |

Also check **your castor's own bolt pattern** against `CASTOR_HOLE_PITCH`
(assumed 20 mm square). Castors vary more than anything else here.

With the assumed figures: deck underside sits **42 mm** above the floor, the line
sensor adjusts between **8 and 24 mm**, and the ultrasonic sits **36 mm** up.

## Why so few exact dimensions

Almost nothing here is a precise fit, deliberately:

- **Motors are held by zip ties through slots**, not bolts through holes, so the
  motor's exact size does not matter.
- **The deck is a grid of M3 clearance holes**, so the ESP32, driver, RFID reader
  and battery bolt down wherever they happen to fit.
- **The sensor bracket uses vertical slots**, so the line sensor's height above
  the floor is set by sliding it. That is the one dimension that has to be right
  and the one nobody gets right first time.

A chassis with exact holes for a motor that turns out two millimetres different
is scrap. This one is not.

## Printing

| | |
|---|---|
| Material | PLA |
| Layer height | 0.2 mm |
| Infill | 20–25% |
| Supports | **None needed** — the parts are oriented for it |
| Perimeters | 3 on the deck, so the zip-tie slots do not tear out |

The bracket prints foot-down with the arm pointing up: a flat base and a vertical
wall, no overhangs. Do not let a slicer "optimise" the orientation — laid on its
back, the foot becomes a large overhang and the support has to be picked out of
the sensor slots afterwards.

## Assembly order

1. **Castor first**, with the riser, and check the deck sits level on a flat
   surface with the wheels on. Adjust the riser height and re-print if not — it
   is a 20-minute print and everything else depends on it.
2. **Motors**, zip-tied through the slot pairs. Pull them tight; a motor that can
   rotate in its mount makes the line following untunable and you will blame the
   PID for days.
3. **Sensor bracket**, bolted to the deck's front edge. Leave the line sensor's
   screws loose for now.
4. **Set the sensor height** with the chair on the actual track surface. Slide it
   until the readings are crisp between black tape and white board, then tighten.
5. Everything else bolts to the grid wherever it fits.

## Building it all in MDF instead

You can make the whole thing from 4 mm MDF, and for the deck that is arguably the
better choice: MDF is stiffer than PLA, cheaper, and cutting a flat plate is a
20-minute job with a hand saw and a drill.

`mdf_template.pdf` is a **1:1 drilling template**. Print it at 100% with page
scaling off, check the ruler on the sheet against a real one, glue it to the
board with a pritt stick, and drill through the crosses. Run
`generate_mdf_template.py` to rebuild it after changing any dimension; it reads
them from `generate_chassis.py`, so the two versions cannot drift apart.

Two things change against the printed version:

- **Slots become pairs of round holes.** The printed deck uses slots for the
  motor zip ties. A slot is awkward by hand, so the template gives two 4 mm holes
  instead — the tie goes down one and up the other, and the result is identical.
- **The bracket becomes two flat pieces.** MDF will not bend, so the arm and foot
  are separate and joined at 90° with wood glue and two small angle brackets from
  any hardware shop. Do not screw into the edge of 4 mm MDF; it splits.

The **castor riser** is the one part that stays awkward. Easiest answer is not to
make it at all: a stack of M3 nylon standoffs is a couple of rupees, comes in
fixed heights, and is adjustable by swapping them.

Practical notes: use a brad-point or sharp twist bit and back the board with
scrap so the underside does not blow out, and **wear a mask** — MDF dust is
genuinely unpleasant.

If you do print, the parts worth printing are the **sensor bracket** and the
**riser**, which are the awkward shapes. The deck is the easy bit either way.

## The wheelchair body — also MDF

`mdf_body_template.pdf` is the second 1:1 sheet: the part that makes it read as a
wheelchair rather than a box with wheels. That matters more than it sounds. A
judge who has to be told what they are looking at has already stopped being
impressed.

Four shapes, six pieces, all flat:

| Piece | Size | |
|---|---|---|
| Side panel × 2 | 120 × 70 | Stepped: armrest at the rear, seat ledge forward. One piece instead of a panel plus a separate armrest — two fewer glued joints to get out of square. |
| Seat pan | 100 × 70 | Rests on cleats, not on the panels — see below. |
| Backrest | 100 × 66 | |
| Footplate | 90 × 25 | |
| Gusset × 4 | 20 mm triangles | Glue does the work; the bolts hold it square while it dries. |
| Seat cleat × 2 | 70 × 10 | Glued inside each side panel at the seat line. Without these the seat has nowhere to sit: the panels are full height where the seat goes, so it cannot rest on top of them. |

### Scale

The deck is 110 mm wide and a wheelchair is about 650 mm across, so this is
**1:6**, and every dimension is a real one divided by six. Assembled:

| | Model | At 1:6 | A real wheelchair |
|---|---|---|---|
| Seat height | 90 mm | 540 mm | ~500 mm |
| Armrest height | 116 mm | 696 mm | ~700 mm |
| Backrest top | 156 mm | 936 mm | ~900 mm |

Keeping the proportions true matters even where nobody would measure: a model
whose proportions are right looks right, and nobody can say why.

The **drive wheels are the wheelchair's rear wheels** — the 65 mm BO wheels sit
just outboard of the side panels, where a wheelchair's wheels belong. The
chassis castor shows at the front as the wheelchair's front castor. Nothing extra
to make, and nothing cosmetic that does not also do a job.

### Assembly

1. **Dry-fit the entire body** before any glue. Every joint, with tape. This is
   the step people skip and the one that saves the build.
2. Side panels onto the deck, square, with gussets inside the corners.
3. Seat pan onto the step. Backrest against the rear edge of the seat.
4. Footplate last, on a gusset off the deck's front.

### Material

Everything on both sheets fits a single **300 × 300 mm piece of 4 mm MDF**,
tightly. 400 × 300 gives room for the one you cut wrong.

The body adds roughly **170 g**, so the loaded chair is around 600 g. The BO
motors are geared and will not notice, but it is another reason to keep the top
speed down.
